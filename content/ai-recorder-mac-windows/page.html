AI Recorders on Mac & Windows (2026): Desktop Apps, Web Apps, and Workarounds

PLAUD shipped a desktop meeting-notetaker app in January 2026; HiDock sells a dock built for Mac mini; iFLYTEK works with no phone at all. But most of this category is still phone-first — here's what actually runs on your computer, device by device.

The category was built phone-first: a slab that pairs with an app in your pocket. That inheritance shows. As of August 2026, exactly one vendor ships a real desktop application, a couple run browser-based web apps, one company sells desktop-shaped hardware, and one device needs no companion device at all. Everyone else's “desktop support” is an export button and a file manager.

That's not a dismissal — for most workflows, phone capture plus cloud sync plus desktop reading is genuinely fine. But if you live in a Mac or Windows machine all day and want to control the recorder from it, this page separates what's real from what's a workaround. It's the desktop half of our compatibility hub.

The short answer

PLAUD for the only real desktop app · iFLYTEK if you want no phone in the loop · HiDock H1 Lite if your recorder should BE the desk setup

PLAUD launched a desktop meeting-notetaker app alongside the NotePin S at CES 2026 [SPEC], with owner reports of it handling Zoom and Teams with Bluetooth headsets [OWNER] — the closest thing this category has to native desktop software. The iFLYTEK SR302 Pro inverts the model: it transcribes on-device and transfers files to your PC, no companion app ever [SPEC]. And HiDock's H1 Lite ($189) is explicitly an “AI audio companion for Mac mini and modern desktops” [SPEC] — a USB-C dock that records.

Most platforms: Omi — iOS, Android, Mac app, and browser [SPEC]

Web app route: PLAUD web app + Notta's browser platform [SPEC]

Desktop-adjacent: HiDock P1 — earphone capture + HiNotes web

Everything else: Phone app + export — see the honest-truth section

The real software

Native desktop apps: PLAUD's meeting notetaker

At CES 2026 (January 4), TechCrunch reported PLAUD launching “a new AI pin and a desktop meeting notetaker” together [REVIEW]; Forbes' CES coverage described the desktop software taking notes at “face-to-face and online meetings” [REVIEW]. The app shipped alongside the NotePin S and appears in our device database as part of its platform list [SPEC].

What it does, per owner reports: captures meeting audio on the computer — including Zoom and Teams calls heard through Bluetooth headsets — and feeds it into the same PLAUD transcription pipeline as the phone app [OWNER]. What it is not: a meeting bot. It doesn't join calls as a participant, doesn't appear in the participant list, and doesn't integrate with Teams as a service — a distinction Facebook PLAUD groups keep asking about (“Is there a way to have it interface directly to Teams?”). The three honest capture methods are laid out in the Zoom/Teams/Meet guide.

Omi deserves the second mention

Omi ships apps for iPhone, Android, Mac, and any browser [SPEC] — making it the broadest platform spread in the category, a side effect of its open-source app ecosystem. If “I want to read and manage transcripts on my Mac” is the whole requirement, Omi's Mac app plus PLAUD's desktop app are the only native options worth the name.

Browser-based

Web apps & the H1 Lite: the middle ground

Two vendors run real web platforms. PLAUD's web app gives you browser access to recordings, transcripts, and summaries on any desktop [SPEC] — the common “review at my desk, capture with my phone or card” workflow. Notta's browser platform is arguably the deepest meeting-software suite attached to any recorder: the Memo card feeds a full web workspace with 58-language transcription, real-time translation, and Business-tier Salesforce/Google Calendar integrations [SPEC].

The hardware twist is HiDock's H1 Lite ($189), pitched at launch as an “AI audio companion for Mac mini and modern desktops” [SPEC] — a USB-C desktop dock with recording and the same lifetime-free HiNotes transcription as the P1 card. It's the only product in the field designed to sit on a desk rather than in a pocket. The P1 card itself pairs with Bluetooth earphones for call capture and manages transcripts through the HiNotes web app [SPEC].

The inverse approach

The no-phone path: iFLYTEK and file transfer

The iFLYTEK SR302 Pro answers the desktop question by deleting the phone from it: transcription runs on the handheld itself (five languages, fully offline), you review transcripts on its 2.0″ screen, and finished files transfer to a PC like any USB storage [SPEC]. No account, no app store, no OS-version anxiety — the trade is cloud-grade English accuracy and AI summaries [REVIEW].

For privacy-constrained environments (legal, medical, government-adjacent), that file-transfer model is a feature rather than a compromise — audio never touches a vendor cloud. Our local vs cloud guide covers when that trade is worth it, and the no-subscription guide covers the economics ($0, forever).

Expectation setting

The honest truth about 'desktop support'

Most AI recorders are phone-first companions, and their desktop workflows are export/import-shaped: record anywhere → sync to the phone → export a file (or push through Notion/Zapier/Dropbox) → do the real work at your desk. That pipeline is exactly what PLAUD's integration surface optimizes — and it's covered in depth in the exports & integrations guide.

What you generally can't do on desktop, August 2026: initiate a recording on the wearable/card from your computer, configure devices over USB, or run batch transcription of external audio files without a phone somewhere in the loop (Notta's web platform being the main exception, since it's a full transcription service that happens to sell hardware). If your workflow genuinely requires desktop-first control — a podcast desk, a lab bench, a studio — the H1 Lite and iFLYTEK paths are the honest shortlist; everyone else should plan around the phone.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is there a PLAUD desktop app for Mac and Windows?
Yes — PLAUD launched a desktop meeting-notetaker app in January 2026 alongside the NotePin S. Owner reports say it captures Zoom and Teams meetings and calls through Bluetooth headsets, feeding the same transcription pipeline as the mobile app. There's also a PLAUD web app for browser access to transcripts on any desktop.
Can I use an AI recorder without a phone?
Recording, yes — every device captures audio standalone. Transcription and management without a phone is rarer: the iFLYTEK SR302 Pro does everything on-device and transfers files to a PC; Omi runs on Mac and browser; PLAUD and Notta offer web apps that can take over once audio has synced somewhere.
Which AI recorder is best for a desktop-only workflow?
Two honest answers. If 'desktop-only' means controlling everything from your computer: HiDock's H1 Lite ($189) is a USB-C dock built for exactly that, and iFLYTEK's handhelds need no companion device at all. If it means 'capture happens elsewhere, but I work at my desk': any device plus PLAUD-class exports (Notion, Zapier, Dropbox, ChatGPT via MCP) covers the review-and-file half.
Does the PLAUD desktop app join Zoom meetings as a participant?
No. It captures meeting audio on the computer — including what you hear through Bluetooth headsets — but it never appears in the participant list or integrates with Teams as a service. If you need a visible, invited notetaker bot, that's software like Otter; if you want unobtrusive capture, the desktop app or a recorder near your speakers is the hardware answer.
Can I transcribe existing audio files on my computer with these devices?
Generally no — the recorders transcribe what their microphones capture (plus their own recordings), not arbitrary files you upload. The main exception is Notta's web platform, which is a full transcription service that happens to pair with the Memo hardware. For bulk file transcription, dedicated software or a local Whisper setup is the better tool — our exports guide covers the DIY route.
