AI Recorder Privacy: Where Your Recordings Actually Go, Device by Device

Every tracked recorder stores your audio locally — the privacy question is what happens at transcription. Here's the honest map: which devices upload, which never do, what PLAUD's server regions mean, and what the Limitless shutdown taught us about companies dying with your transcripts.

“Where do my recordings go?” is the question AI recorder buyers ask second — right after price — and the one manufacturers answer least clearly. The marketing says “privacy-first recording”; the reality is a split architecture: capture is local on every device we track, and transcription is where your audio leaves.

This page maps that split, device by device, with each claim labeled by evidence tier. We don't do hands-on testing — we read the manufacturers' own support docs, independent reviews, and owner reports, and we tell you which is which. If you're deciding what's legal rather than what's private, that's a different page: our consent-laws guide.

The short answer

iFLYTEK SR302 Pro if audio must never leave · Omi for local on-phone processing · everyone else uploads at transcription

Recording is stored on the device and paired app everywhere. The privacy difference happens at transcription: iFLYTEK processes entirely on the handheld — no account, no cloud [SPEC]. Omi runs unlimited transcription locally on your phone [SPEC]. Every other tracked device — PLAUD, TicNote, Notta, Pocket, soundcore, HiDock — uploads audio to company servers for transcription. Neither branch is wrong; only you know which conversations can afford the upload.

Hybrid: Omi — local by default, cloud optional

Reported: soundcore Work ~12 GB offline model [OWNER, unconfirmed]

Record-only: Any device used without transcribing stays local

The core distinction

Recording is local. Transcription may not be.

Two separate processes get blurred in marketing. Recording — microphone to storage — happens on the device, offline, on every AI recorder we track. Your conversations sit in flash memory, metered by nobody: a 64 GB store holds hundreds of hours, and no subscription is needed to keep recording.

Transcription — audio to text — is the fork. Cloud devices upload the audio file, process it on servers, and return a transcript. Local devices run a smaller model on the hardware itself. From a privacy standpoint, everything that matters happens at this fork: it determines whether your voice ever crosses the internet, whether a company holds your audio even briefly, and whether your archive survives a company's death. The full accuracy/cost side of that fork is covered in local vs cloud transcription, and the pipeline it sits inside is mapped in how AI voice recorders work.

The nuance in 'local recording'

On cloud devices, the audio file typically lives on the recorder and your phone app. PLAUD's support docs state recordings are stored on the device and app by default, with cloud copies only if you enable Private Cloud Sync — and that no persistent copy remains on PLAUD servers after transcription when sync is off [SPEC via support docs]. That's a meaningful commitment. It is still a categorically different promise from “the audio never leaves the device.”

Case study

PLAUD in detail, because it's the market leader

PLAUD is the device most readers own or are considering, and its documentation is the most specific in the category — worth reading closely:

  • Default storage: recordings stay on the device and the paired app. Cloud storage applies only if you enable Private Cloud Sync [SPEC via support docs].
  • Transcription processing: happens on PLAUD servers, and the company's EU privacy-security page specifies regional data centers in the United States, Germany, Japan, and Singapore — your region determines which [SPEC via support docs]. A Japanese owner review independently reported AWS Tokyo storage for Japan accounts, which is consistent [OWNER].
  • Retention: with Cloud Sync disabled, PLAUD states no persistent copy of your audio is stored on their servers after transcription [SPEC via support docs]. The transient processing window still means the audio was uploaded — that's the fork, working as described.
  • The reviewer's verdict: a third-party review of the Note Pro concluded that “if absolute privacy is crucial, you might need to look elsewhere or stick to local-only recording” [REVIEW — mightygadget].

For sensitive work, the practical takeaway isn't “avoid PLAUD" — it's knowing the architecture: PLAUD offers a record-only mode too. Since recording never requires transcription, you can capture on any cloud device and transcribe selectively — only the files you choose to process ever leave. Devices with bigger free transcription allowances make that discipline harder to keep, which is one honest argument for choosing a metered plan.

The cautionary tale

The Limitless lesson: what happens when the company dies

In December 2025, Limitless stopped selling its $99 pendant — one of the first popular AI recorders — and committed to supporting existing customers “throughout 2026” [SPEC — limitless.ai]. After that window, the cloud service that transcribed everything is expected to end.

What this teaches: your recorder's lifespan is not the hardware's lifespan. A device whose transcription depends on a company's servers has a support-ticket-shaped expiry date. Recording still works — the files are yours — but the “AI” half of the product is rented infrastructure that a shutdown, acquisition, or pivot can turn off. When buyers ask “what happens if the company shuts down?” — the Limitless owners' experience is the answer.

What we know — and don't — about Meta

Headlines after the discontinuation implied a connection between Limitless and Meta (“After the Meta…”-style coverage). We could not verify any acquisition or corporate relationship: it is unconfirmed, and we say exactly that rather than repeat the implication. What is confirmed: the product is discontinued and the support window runs throughout 2026 [SPEC — limitless.ai].

The defensive buyers' moves, in order of effectiveness: prefer devices whose core recording and file export work standalone (all tracked devices pass that bar); prefer local transcription (iFLYTEK) where the whole pipeline survives a corporate event; and keep your audio exported somewhere you control — the strongest subscription-free survivors are rounded up in our no-subscription guide, and the export habits are covered in our exports guide.

The other privacy

Recording other people: the ethics note

Device privacy — where your audio goes — is half the subject. The other half is the people in your recordings, and here the professional consensus is blunt: employment lawyers and workplace-privacy publications now specifically flag discreet AI recorders as a workplace risk, because the same compactness that makes a coin-sized recorder convenient makes covert recording easy [REVIEW — Workplace Privacy Report, Reed Smith, JD Supra, 2025–2026].

  • Recording a colleague, client, or patient can implicate consent law — see our consent-laws guide for the one-party vs all-party framework (educational, not legal advice).
  • Attorneys' meetings raise privilege concerns when AI tools record them [REVIEW — Duane Morris, Feb 2026].
  • Employees are actively researching how to refuse colleagues' AI note-takers — the people being recorded are part of this market too [OWNER — r/privacy].

Our stance, which shapes every guide on this site: disclosure is the durable strategy. A visible recorder and a sentence of consent protects you legally and produces better meetings than discovery later.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Are my recordings stored in the cloud by default?
No — on every tracked device, recordings are stored on the device and paired app by default. What uploads is the audio being transcribed, and on PLAUD specifically, cloud copies exist only if you enable Private Cloud Sync [SPEC via support docs]. The distinction matters: your archive is local; your processing may not be.
Which AI recorder is the most private?
By architecture, the iFLYTEK SR302 Pro: transcription runs entirely on the handheld with no account, no upload, and no subscription [SPEC]. Omi is second — unlimited transcription runs locally on your phone [SPEC]. Everything else uploads audio at transcription time. Note we don't hands-on-test; this is manufacturers' documented architecture, labeled by tier.
Where are PLAUD recordings processed?
On PLAUD's cloud servers, in regional data centers in the United States, Germany, Japan, or Singapore depending on your region [SPEC via support docs — eu.plaud.ai privacy page]. With cloud sync disabled, PLAUD states no persistent server copy is retained after transcription.
What happens to my recordings if the company shuts down?
Your local audio files survive — recording never depended on the company. What you lose is the service layer: transcription, summaries, sync. The Limitless Pendant is the live example: discontinued December 2025, customers supported 'throughout 2026' [SPEC]. Local-transcription devices (iFLYTEK) and open-source options (Omi) degrade most gracefully.
Is a 'private version of Plaud' possible?
Owners are actively asking for one on Reddit, and the building blocks exist — local Whisper-class models run on modern phones and laptops. What's missing as of August 2026 is a polished commercial product bundling it. Omi's on-phone transcription is the closest shipping answer; iFLYTEK covers the fully-offline case.
Does GDPR change which device I should buy?
GDPR concerns lawful basis and handling of voice data — a legal question our consent-laws guide frames but cannot answer for you. Architecturally, local-transcription devices simply minimize the personal-data exposure, which is the direction privacy regulators tend to favor.
