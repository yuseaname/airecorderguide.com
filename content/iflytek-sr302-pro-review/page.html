iFLYTEK SR302 Pro Review (2026): The Fully Offline Handheld, Honestly Weighed

AI Recorder Guide editorial team·Fact-checked September 2026·Updated Sep 12, 2026·9 min read

The short version

The 50-word verdict

The iFLYTEK SR302 Pro is the category's purest offline pitch: real-time transcription on the handheld itself, in five maker-named languages, with no subscription and no cloud stop. You trade away the app ecosystem, AI summaries, and — per the independent review we cite — some English accuracy. Privacy-first buyers should shortlist it.

The details

The offline pitch

Every AI recorder we track records locally; the decision that matters is what happens next — upload for transcription, or an on-device model does the work. The SR302 Pro — sold on iFLYTEK's own store as the "Smart Recorder, Support Offline Voice Transcription in Real Time" — commits fully to the second branch: "Full Offline Transcription — Real-time transcription without the need for internet" [SPEC — store.iflytek.com, retrieved September 12, 2026], with feature tabs compressing the promise to "10m Audio Recording · 5 Major Languages Offline Transcription · AI Noise Reduction".

The language menu is specific: the maker states the recorder "supports multi-language transcription in Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, Russian, and other languages" — five named languages. That is a short list beside the 112-language menus on cloud leaders — the first honest cost of the offline branch: breadth traded for the guarantee that audio never leaves the hardware.

Getting work off the device is deliberately low-tech: the maker advertises "convenient and secure data transfer to a computer via USB flash drive" — plug in and copy files, no sync service or vendor server in the middle. Our local vs cloud transcription guide frames the trade-off triangle — privacy, accuracy, cost, pick two — and positions the SR302 Pro as its anchor product for the privacy corner; our privacy guide maps where each device's audio goes.

One honesty note: the maker's store page never mentions an account, a companion app, or a subscription — the design implies a standalone tool, and our local-vs-cloud guide treats it as account-free. But iFLYTEK publishes no explicit "no account required" statement, so we phrase it as maker-silent; verify at purchase if it matters to you.

Going deeper

What the hardware offers

The hardware story is a microphone array first: "2 directional microphones and 4 omnidirectional microphones, supporting recordings up to 10m", with "360-degree omnidirectional sound pickup, ensuring clear sound captured without dead angle". Ten meters is a maker claim, not an independent measurement — pickup at distance depends on room acoustics and placement, as our mic-distance guide explains.

Around the array: intelligent noise reduction via "iFLYTEK's intelligent noise reduction algorithm" — from the company that markets "25 years in speech recognition" — and an "8-core processor for faster transcription speed" behind the maker's boldest number: "Transcribe 1 hour of audio in just 5 minutes". That twelve-times-real-time claim, produced entirely on the handheld, is what makes the offline pitch credible.

The physical product is a handheld with a "2-inch touchscreen, providing a more convenient and user-friendly use" and "4 recording modes, covering meetings, speeches, and other common scenarios" — on a standalone device with no phone app, that screen is the only interface. Four modes is a modest menu — meetings and speeches are named; the other two are not.

All of the above is manufacturer-published. We compared published specifications, owner reports, and plan structures; we have not hands-on-tested the device, and no independent benchmark of the mic array or transcription engine exists in our dataset.

Practical guidance

Endurance and storage honesty

Battery is where our dataset needed a correction, so we will be explicit. The maker's store page claims "Recording up to 7 hours… without interruption", and its FAQ repeats: "up to 7 hours of continuous recording". Our dataset previously carried a longer battery figure — a 20-hour claim not published on the maker's store page — now corrected to the maker's own number. Seven hours covers a day of meetings, not a multi-day conference without a recharge; iFLYTEK publishes no mAh capacity to cross-check.

Storage is more generous: 32 GB holds "approximately 175 hours of recordings" per the maker, with the FAQ confirming "the total recording time is up to 175 hours" — months of audio at typical meeting volumes, archived by copying files over USB, not trusting a vendor's retention policy.

The per-file ceiling is the detail most listings omit. The maker's FAQ states "the maximum duration of each recording file is 5 hours", and a second FAQ line gives the practical figure: "the longest recording time of 4 hours and 55 minutes," after which "the recording will stop to save the file and start recording a new audio automatically". A marathon session will not be lost, but it arrives as sequential files with a seam roughly every five hours — plan for the stitch.

The wrap-up

Who it is for

This is a tool for people whose constraint is not features but where the audio is allowed to exist. If you sit in confidential meetings — board minutes, HR conversations, client matters, anything under a no-cloud policy — the transcript is produced on hardware you control and the audio never touches a vendor's server. That is the branch our privacy guide maps device by device, and why privacy-first professions, lawyers among them, keep surfacing offline recorders; the local vs cloud guide calls this device the standalone answer for that buyer.

Journalists in source-protection situations are the second audience: offline transcription produces no server-side copy of a sensitive interview to breach or misroute — a categorically different posture from cloud-first recorders, however good their encryption. Our journalists' guide covers that workflow, and one boundary bears repeating: local processing changes where audio goes, not whether recording is lawful — consent rules still apply (recording consent laws guide).

The third audience is the subscription refuser: offline transcription is a device feature, not a metered service — no GPU bill to pass on, which is why iFLYTEK can sell transcription as a one-time inclusion. Our no-subscription guide compares every subscription-free path; this is the purest.

Context

What you give up

The trade is wider than most offline pitches admit. No app ecosystem — no companion app store, no third-party tools; the maker's page describes a recorder, not a platform. No integrations — nothing flows to Notion, ChatGPT, Zapier, or a CRM, because there is no cloud account to connect. And no cloud summaries: the summary-and-chat layer is cloud-model territory, and the maker's store page does not describe one. What you receive is a transcript file — a genuine gap if your workflow starts, not ends, at the transcript.

English accuracy is the second cost, and it is documented: our local vs cloud guide cites a third-party review of this device — mightygadget, March 2025 — that found the offline English transcription "noticeably less precise" than phone-based cloud services, particularly with accents and complex terminology. On-device models are smaller by design; assume more cleanup on varied-speaker English meetings.

Owner evidence is the third gap, and we will not paper over it: as of our September 12, 2026 check, independent English-market owner reports are thin — our research pass could not surface a meaningful base, and Amazon listing signals are listing facts, not evidence. You would be buying on the maker's claims plus one independent review signal; our review methodology labels this page evidence synthesis with a wide unknown column.

Two smaller silences: iFLYTEK does not publish a translation claim for this device — the store page promises multi-language transcription, not translation — so treat translation as unconfirmed. And the battery's capacity is unpublished, which is why the endurance section leans on the maker's 7-hour figure.

The SR302 Pro is sold direct through the maker's own store, which advertises a "30-Day Return & Refund Guarantee" — "a full refund within 30 days after your delivery date" — a 1-year warranty, and free shipping to the US, UK, and Canada (2–9 business days, non-remote regions) [SPEC — store policies]; check current price and the return window before ordering.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does the SR302 Pro need an internet connection to transcribe?
No — that is the maker's core claim: "Full Offline Transcription — Real-time transcription without the need for internet". Transcription runs on the handheld's 8-core processor — the reason the maker can include transcription free rather than metering it as a plan.
Does it require an account or a companion app?
The maker's store page never mentions an account, an app, or a subscription; the design — on-device transcription plus USB transfer — implies a standalone tool, and our local-vs-cloud guide treats it as account-free. But iFLYTEK publishes no explicit "no account required" statement, so we flag it as maker-silent; verify with the seller if you need written confirmation.
How long does the battery actually last?
The maker's own store page says up to 7 hours of continuous recording. Longer figures — 20 hours — circulate in third-party datasets but are not on the maker's store page; we have corrected our dataset to the maker's figure. iFLYTEK publishes no mAh capacity, so 7 hours is the only maker-backed number available.
Which languages does offline transcription support — and how good is the English?
Five named languages: Chinese, English, Japanese, Korean, and Russian, with the maker adding "and other languages". On English quality, the independent signal is unflattering: a third-party review cited in our local-vs-cloud guide found offline English "noticeably less precise" than cloud services, especially with accents and technical terms. Plan on editing if English accuracy is mission-critical.
Can it translate between languages?
iFLYTEK does not publish a translation claim for this recorder: the store page promises multi-language real-time transcription — text in the language spoken — not translation between languages. Treat translation as unconfirmed; if you need it, cloud rivals are the branch that documents it.
What is the return policy and warranty?
Buying direct from iFLYTEK's store, the maker advertises a 30-day satisfaction guarantee — full refund within 30 days of delivery — a 1-year warranty, and free shipping to the US, UK, and Canada (2–9 business days, non-remote regions). If you prefer Amazon's return window, see the sibling note above — and match the model number before ordering.
