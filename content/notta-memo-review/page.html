Notta Memo Review 2026: Behind Notta’s App-First Brand

Notta Memo is the ultra-thin card recorder that sits behind Notta's well-known transcription app — a $149 magnetic card that is cheap, card-like, and multilingual. The honest caveat is that almost nobody independent has reviewed it in English yet. Here is our evidence synthesis: what manufacturer specs claim, what the cost math looks like, and which gaps you should know about before you buy.

The short answer

A cheap, card-like multilingual recorder — if you already live in the Notta app and accept a thin independent track record

The Notta Memo is a $149 magnetic card (the shop.notta.ai listing bundles it with a magnetic leather protective case [SPEC]) that weighs 28 g and is 0.35 cm thin [SPEC]. It shines as a lightweight companion to Notta's transcription app and speaks 58 languages [SPEC], and the free tier gives you 120 minutes a month [SUBDOC]. But it is cloud-only, its call mode is placement-based, and no major English outlet has reviewed it yet [REVIEW context] — so if you need true call capture or a proven independent record, the PLAUD Note or TicNote are safer bets.

The app ecosystem angle

The Notta Memo's main selling point is that it is the hardware half of an app-first brand. The card records, then the Notta app transcribes, summarizes, and routes the output. That matters if you already run Notta for meetings and interviews — the hardware and software are built to work together.

The manufacturer claims one-click export to hundreds of platforms, including Slack and Salesforce [SPEC], with Notion, Zoom, and calendar sync also listed [SPEC]. These are manufacturer claims, not independently verified integrations — but if your workflow already uses these tools, the Memo is worth a look. Frequently asked questions

Does the Notta Memo capture phone calls?
It switches between two modes via a slide switch: an in-person meeting mode and a smartphone call mode. The call mode relies on a bone-conduction mic inside the magnetic case, so it records both sides of a call only while the card is magnetically mounted against the phone [SPEC]. That is a supported workflow, but it is placement-based rather than true call capture, and it works only while the card is physically sitting against the phone.
How much does the Notta Memo cost over three years?
About $442.97 on our dataset: the $149 hardware bundle (the shop.notta.ai listing is the Notta Memo with a magnetic leather protective case) plus three years of Pro at $97.99/yr if the 120 free minutes ever run short. That works out to roughly $4.54 per 1,000 transcription minutes on the Pro plan. Prices move, so treat these as August 2026 editorial research and verify the live price before buying.
Do I need a subscription to use the Notta Memo?
No. The free tier includes 120 transcription minutes per month, and recording and storage continue regardless; you only pay $97.99/yr for Pro if you outgrow the meter and want 1,800 minutes per month plus extra cloud AI features. Those Pro terms are dated August 2026 and were not re-confirmed on notta.ai on 2026-09-05.
What does the Notta Memo free tier cover?
120 transcription minutes per month on the free tier, per the shop.notta.ai listing [SPEC]. Everything else — transcription, summaries, mind maps, the 30+ summary templates — is cloud-based through the Notta app, and there is no offline transcription mode; see our local vs cloud guide for the trade-off.
Is the Notta Memo transcription accurate?
Manufacturer specs claim 58 languages, 98.86% speaker-identification accuracy, and up to 98% transcription accuracy [SPEC]. Independent benchmarking in that detail is what is missing from the category: no major English outlet has published a lab-style comparison, so treat these as manufacturer claims to verify against your own recordings, not independently confirmed results.
