PLAUD vs Pocket (2026): Which Budget Recorder Actually Costs Less?

How we rank and what we won't claim: review methodology.

PLAUD is the category leader with 300 free minutes and a $239.99/yr Unlimited tier. Pocket is a budget challenger claiming unlimited transcription with no subscription. One of them is the safest buy in the category — and the other is the best value if its claims hold. Here's the honest breakdown of both.

This is the market's sharpest philosophical disagreement, packaged as two credit-card-sized recorders. PLAUD — the category leader, #1 on Amazon's AI note-taker charts [SPEC] — includes 300 free transcription minutes a month and sells Pro at $99.99/yr and Unlimited at $239.99/yr. Pocket by HeyPocket — currently #2 on that same Amazon chart — sells for $99 at launch ($199 list) and claims unlimited standard-accuracy transcription with no subscription required [SPEC].

Related: PLAUD vs Bee · the full PLAUD Note review.

The short version: Pocket’s own marketing contradicts itself — and where it does decides this buy. Reddit buyers already cross-shop them constantly (“Considering Plaud or Pocket for daily quick notes” — r/PLAUDAI). The section below shows exactly where Pocket’s numbers stop agreeing with each other.

The short answer

PLAUD if you want the proven ecosystem · Pocket if you're subscription-averse, risk-tolerant, and verify terms at purchase

These are different buyers' devices, not rivals on one ladder. PLAUD's case is maturity: the deepest export and integration surface in the category (Notion, Zapier, Dropbox, ChatGPT via MCP), proven iPhone call capture, and retail availability with easy returns. Pocket's case is economics: ~$99 total three-year cost if the unlimited-transcription claim holds — versus $159–879 for a PLAUD Note depending on tier. The asterisk is trust, below.

The trust conflict, up front

Pocket's own pages disagree about what's free

HeyPocket's homepage and marketing pages promise “Unlimited minutes • No subscription required” for standard-accuracy transcription [SPEC]. Another heypocket.com page describes a free plan of “300 transcription minutes per month” — the same allowance PLAUD gives away. The contradiction is unresolved as of August 2026. Until it is, treat “unlimited” as a claim to verify at checkout, not a settled fact.

The second data point is community sentiment. Pocket's Trustpilot reviews are positive on ease of use [REVIEW], but Reddit carries an active warning thread:

The verdict

Who should buy which

Buy PLAUD if

  • You want the proven ecosystem — native Notion export, Zapier, Dropbox, ChatGPT MCP access [SPEC]
  • Recording iPhone calls is part of the plan — the vibration-sensor case is the category's most proven method [SPEC]
  • Easy returns matter — Amazon, Best Buy, B&H, Sam's Club all stock it [SPEC]
  • You want a brand with two years of public owner sentiment, warts included [OWNER]

Buy Pocket if

  • Subscription cost is your core objection and you accept new-brand risk in exchange
  • You'd verify the current free-minute terms on heypocket.com (or in-cart) at purchase time
  • On-device display matters more than integrations — InstantView shows status without the app [SPEC]
  • You use under ~300 minutes a month — the tier where either device's free allowance suffices

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Pocket's unlimited transcription really free?
That's the unresolved question. HeyPocket's homepage says 'Unlimited minutes • No subscription required' for standard-accuracy transcription; another page on the same site mentions 300 free minutes per month. Trustpilot reviews are positive, but a Reddit thread titled 'Avoid Pocket, I guess' raises trust concerns. Our position: treat it as a strong claim to verify at purchase, not a fact — and know the return window before buying.
Do I need a subscription to use PLAUD?
No. Every PLAUD device includes the free Starter plan with 300 transcription minutes per month, and recording itself is unlimited. You only pay if you exceed 300 minutes and want AI transcription of the excess — Pro ($99.99/yr, 1,200 min/mo) or Unlimited ($239.99/yr), plus add-on minute packs.
What happens if I go over 300 minutes on PLAUD?
Recording keeps working — it's unlimited. AI transcription pauses until next month unless you upgrade to Pro or Unlimited or buy an Extra Minutes pack (3,000 min for $59.99 or 6,000 min for $99.99, valid 24 months). Also note the 5-hour per-file transcription cap regardless of tier.
Is Pocket just a PLAUD clone?
Not literally — HeyPocket is a separate company (PLAUD even published its own review of the device). The hardware formula is similar: a thin magnetic card with 112-language cloud transcription. Differences that matter: Pocket's InstantView display, its claimed unlimited free standard transcription, and a much thinner integration ecosystem than PLAUD's.
Which is better for a student on a budget?
On pure price with the unlimited claim taken at face value, Pocket. With the claim discounted, the stronger play is usually TicNote — 600 free minutes a month covers a typical course load (5–10 hrs/month) with room to spare, and Pro is $79/yr if you outgrow it. Run your course schedule through the minutes calculator before deciding; the student guide has the full math.
