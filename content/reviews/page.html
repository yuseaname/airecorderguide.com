Every device we review gets the same treatment: dated manufacturer specs, the independent coverage that actually exists, owner reports with threads named, and the three-year cost math — tier-tagged claim by claim. No hands-on testing claims, ever.

The short version: the PLAUD Note is the category’s default choice for good reasons — true phone-call capture, a mature app with real integrations, and 60-day standby — but it is also the category’s most aggressive subscription meter. If your month fits inside 300 free minutes, it is excellent value. If it does not, the $99.99/yr Pro plan quietly doubles its price — and rivals like TicNote include twice the minutes for $21 less per year. This review synthesizes owner reports and independent tests; we have not hands-on-tested the device.

Frequently asked questions

Do you test the devices yourself?
No. Every review on this site is evidence synthesis — manufacturer pages, named independent reviews, and owner communities, each dated and tagged. We never claim hands-on testing.
Which review should I start with?
The one that matches your budget question: the Note Pro for the flagship decision, TicNote for value, HiDock P1 if the answer must be no-subscription, NotePin S for wearables. The hubs route by use case.
Why do some reviews say coverage is thin?
Because it is. When almost nobody independent has reviewed a device (Notta Memo in English, currently), the honest review says so — that disclosure is part of the method, not an apology.
