Where TicNote wins

Twice the free minutes, cheaper plan. 600 free minutes a month is the largest allowance we track, and the $79 Pro plan undercuts PLAUD’s by $21 a year while carrying the larger Pro allowance in our dataset — 2,100 vs 1,200 min/mo [SPEC] (TicNote does not publish the Pro allowance on its own pages as of Sep 2026; the 2,100 figure is our dataset value — see the honesty note below). Over three years the totals separate by roughly $93.

The entry price. At a $99 street-price floor, TicNote is one of the cheapest credible devices in the category — the budget entry that doesn’t skip transcription.

An empty SERP, honestly noted. Independent reviews of TicNote are still rare — search results are dominated by the brand’s own pages. Our TicNote review says plainly what is verified versus unknown.

Where PLAUD Note wins

True phone-call capture. The magnetic case records calls directly — the workaround most iPhone owners actually want [SPEC]. TicNote’s placement-only method works but demands positioning discipline.

The integration stack. Notion, ChatGPT via MCP server, Zapier, Dropbox [SPEC] — the most mature export story in the category.

More independent scrutiny. The Note is the most-covered device in independent tests and owner reports — see the full review — which means its failure modes are documented, not discovered by you.

The phone-call difference, in practice

"Placement only" means the device captures a call by sitting against your phone’s speaker — usable, but sensitive to positioning and room noise. "True call capture" means the device records the call itself through its case mount. For journalists and sales reps who live on calls, this alone justifies the Note’s premium. The full ladder of call-recording methods — and the consent-law map that governs them — is in our phone-call guide and consent-law explainer.

Pick by user type

  • Consultants and heavy meeting-days: TicNote — 600 free minutes absorbs a brutal week before any plan starts.
  • Anyone whose work is phone calls: PLAUD Note — true capture beats placement, every time.
  • Notion/ChatGPT pipeline builders: PLAUD Note — the MCP export is unique at this price [SPEC].
  • Budget-first buyers: TicNote, then verify the current street price — the floor moves.

Where the money actually goes

Strip the brand names and this is a $93 bet on two questions. Question one: do you exceed 300 minutes a month? If no, the PLAUD Note’s free tier serves you identically to TicNote’s 600 — both cost hardware only — and the cheaper entry price favors TicNote anyway. If yes, both push you onto Pro, and TicNote’s plan is cheaper every single year.

Question two: how often do you record calls? Placement-only capture works for the occasional call in a quiet room. It fails exactly when you need it — moving, in a car, in noise. True capture through PLAUD’s case is the difference between a tool that sometimes works and one that always does.

The spreadsheet answer is TicNote. The workflow answer depends entirely on question two. That is not a dodge — it is the actual decision, and it is why we publish the full three-year dataset instead of a single pick.

One more honesty note: TicNote’s independent review coverage remains thin — brand pages dominate its search results — so its reliability record is largely unverified. PLAUD’s is documented, including its failures. Some buyers should pay for the known quantity.

A buying strategy that removes the risk

Whichever device you choose, buy for the free tier first and the plan second. Run your real month through the minutes calculator; if the answer fits either device’s free allowance, buy the cheaper hardware and revisit in ninety days with your actual usage data in hand.

Verify the current street price and bundle contents on Amazon before checkout — our figures are August 2026 research and this category reprices monthly. And keep the return window honest: a week of real meetings tells you more about mic reach and app fit than any spec table can.

TicNote vs the PLAUD Note Pro: the $90 gap

If your PLAUD candidate is the Note Pro ($189) rather than the base Note, the math shifts twice. Hardware: the gap widens to $90 at list [RETAIL, Aug 2026], and the Pro answers TicNote’s mic advantage with a 4 MEMS + 1 VPU array rated to 16.4 ft [SPEC, plaud.ai Sep 2026] against TicNote’s 3 MEMS [SPEC]. Plans: not at all — the Pro runs the same $99.99/1,200 plan as the Note [SUBDOC], so TicNote’s $79/2,100-dataset advantage is unchanged [SPEC, dataset figure — not manufacturer-published]. The honest split: heavy minutes still point at TicNote; big-room capture and the display point at the Pro. FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper over three years?
TicNote: $336 versus $428.97 for the PLAUD Note (hardware at the lower bound plus three years of each Pro plan). The gap is entirely the plan — TicNote’s Pro is $79/yr versus $99.99/yr, and its free tier includes 600 minutes a month versus 300. Full dataset: the TCO study.
Which one records phone calls better?
PLAUD Note, clearly. It captures calls directly through its magnetic case; TicNote is placement-only — it records calls acoustically when held against the phone speaker [SPEC]. If phone interviews are your core use, the Note’s method is the category benchmark: call-recording guide.
Is TicNote’s 600 free minutes really usable?
600 minutes a month is the largest free allowance we track [SPEC] — roughly 15 hour-long meetings. Whether it is *enough* depends on your volume: measure your month first. Note that independent review coverage of TicNote is still thin; our confidence rests on manufacturer terms, not deep third-party testing.
Who should pick which?
TicNote for budget-conscious and high-volume recorders who can live with placement-only calls; PLAUD Note for professionals who need true call capture and mature integrations. The full review of the Note’s trade-offs is in our PLAUD Note evidence review.
