The subscription question

The hardware price is the small number

Nearly every AI recorder now meters transcription: record all you want, but AI minutes cost money. The industry has quietly converged on a 300 free minutes/month starter allowance — and a $239.99/year “Unlimited” tier that costs more than the device itself, every single year.

Three devices break that model: HiDock P1 and Pocket include unlimited transcription, and iFLYTEK's recorders transcribe entirely on-device with no account at all.

What everyone asks first

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Do AI voice recorders require a subscription?
Most don't require one — PLAUD, Mobvoi, soundcore, and Notta all include free monthly transcription allowances (120–600 min). But heavy users hit those caps fast, and 'Unlimited' tiers converge at $239.99/yr. Three devices skip subscriptions entirely: HiDock P1 (unlimited included), Pocket (unlimited standard transcription), and iFLYTEK recorders (offline, no account).
Is PLAUD actually the best AI recorder?
It's the most mature ecosystem and the safest default — best-known iPhone call-capture trick, Notion/ChatGPT export, wide retail availability. But Reddit owners complain about speaker labels and subscription value, and if you record enough to need Unlimited, alternatives like HiDock P1 (no subscription) or TicNote (600 free min + $79/yr Pro) cost far less over three years.
Can these record phone calls on an iPhone?
Yes — that's PLAUD's signature trick. iOS blocks call-recording apps, so the Note's MagSafe case uses a vibration sensor pressed against the phone to capture both sides of a call from the hardware itself. Notta Memo uses a bone-conduction mic for the same effect, and HiDock P1 captures calls routed through Bluetooth earphones. Always check consent law first.
What happens to my recordings — are they private?
Recording is local on every device we track; the privacy question is transcription. Cloud devices (PLAUD, TicNote, Notta, Pocket…) upload audio to company servers for processing. iFLYTEK recorders transcribe entirely on-device, and Omi runs unlimited transcription on your phone. For sensitive conversations, that distinction is the whole ballgame.
Won't ChatGPT's recording feature make these obsolete?
It covers part of the workflow — transcribe and summarize audio you feed it. But it can't record phone calls, drains your phone battery, needs you to open the app and aim a mic, and misses unplanned conversations. Owner reports consistently cite exactly those moments as the reason dedicated hardware exists.
